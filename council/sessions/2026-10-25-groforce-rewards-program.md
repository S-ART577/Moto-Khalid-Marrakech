# GROFORCE rewards program — council session 2026-10-25

## Flat paragraph
GROFORCE is a loyalty and reseller-rewards program run by GROSHOP (groshop.net), a Moroccan online consumer-electronics retailer that sells cash on delivery, holds stock in Morocco and runs its own certified after-sales service. Members earn 10 points per 1 DH spent; points can be redeemed against the full price of any product with no cap; the value of a point at redemption is not yet defined. Planned Bronze/Silver/Golden tiers (thresholds and perks undefined) unlock discounts, early access and priority treatment. A reseller track requires a minimum order of 20 articles with a different perk set; a regular customer reaching an undefined limit can upgrade to it. Expiry, stacking with promos/codes and credit timing are undecided. Site, points infrastructure, an 18-second launch film and tier card designs exist. GROSHOP earns thin margins on electronics.

## Believer
- Pain: (A) small phone/accessory reseller "Youssef", 29, buys 20+ units, 4-6 h/week on wholesaler calls (assumption), no after-sales backing; (B) repeat COD consumer earning nothing back; (C) owner paying ads to reacquire the same customers.
- Today: phone/WhatsApp sourcing, unstable prices, warranty risk absorbed by reseller; GROSHOP's in-country stock + certified after-sales are the reseller perk.
- Why now: site, points infra, film and cards already built; COD/stock/after-sales hard to copy; assumed rising ad costs.
- Best case 18 months (assumptions): 5,000 members, 25% repeat in 6 months, 150 active resellers monthly, Golden card as status object. Conditions: point value < margin, credit on delivery, no stacking, 12-month expiry, Silver reachable by 2nd/3rd purchase, WhatsApp ordering with fixed price sheet, upgrade limit at bulk-buying point.
- Test: 30 repeat + 10 bulk buyers, second order within 45 days.

## Skeptic
- Verdict: FIX FIRST. Resellers want lowest unit price/credit in writing, not points; Derb Ghallef-type informal wholesale is the incumbent; 20-article minimum is a constraint. Consumers cannot value an undefined point; COD buyers compare prices.
- Competitors: no dedicated reseller-rewards program found. Marjane card (10-20% on ~400 products, 2022), FID&You (CMI card points, not COD), Jumia price-match, Electroplanet no loyalty found; real incumbents are manual (WhatsApp price lists, souk contacts, spreadsheets — unverified).
- Buried assumption: customers want a reward over a price cut and it costs less. At 8-10% margin (assumption) a ~1% reward changes nothing; "full price, no cap" + stacking can sell below cost, x20 for resellers. COD guest orders may never reach the program. Electronics repeat slowly.
- Death mode: margin leak plus apathy; ad-driven sign-ups of already-loyal buyers; ~zero reseller sign-ups; rules decided under pressure; COD refusals (10-20%, assumption) force reversals; program frozen.
- Believer weakness: all numbers assumptions, sunk-cost "why now", invented personas, conditions = the unsolved problem, no control group.
- Survives: WhatsApp price sheet with stock + after-sales for bulk buyers, no points.
- Sources: wikipedia Derb_Ghallef, wikipedia Electroplanet, medias24 Marjane card article (2022), fnh.ma FID&You, fnh.ma Societe Generale/Jumia.

## Investor
- Verdict: NO to points program as pitched; conditional small yes to narrow reseller offer.
- Economics (all assumptions): consumer order 1,500 DH, 10% margin, delivery 35 DH, 1% back = 15 DH, 15% COD refusal, ad acquisition ~120 DH; reseller order 2,400 DH, 6% margin, ~80 DH contribution; haircut best case ~83,000 DH (~$8,700) over 18 months.
- First ten customers: export orders, personal WhatsApp to repeat and 20+ article buyers, ask one referral each, visit 5 shops.
- Cheapest test: bulk price sheet to 10 bulk buyers; ask 30 repeat buyers "1% points or 3% off?" with half as no-reward control. Pass: 4/10 reply, 2+ order in 7 days, 3/10 want monthly. Kill: <2 replies or majority want price cut.
- Would not invest: no baseline, uncapped liability, 1% too small, advantage is retail not loyalty.

## Judge
VERDICT: FIX FIRST

WHY: The Investor's argument was strongest because it put numbers on the dispute: a 1% reward (about 15 DH on a 1,500 DH order) is too small to change behavior, and uncapped redemption on a 20-unit order can wipe out the margin. The Believer was weakest because the conditions it listed (point value, expiry, stacking, crediting) are the unsolved problem, and the persona numbers and "why now" are assumptions and sunk cost. The Skeptic was right that the points program does not survive. The Investor was right that a narrow reseller offer might (fixed price sheet, in-country stock, certified after-sales, no points, no tiers), but only if the test numbers show it.

BIGGEST RISK: Neither resellers nor COD consumers want points, because they want a lower price or credit, while an undefined, uncapped redemption on thin 6-10% margins can sell below cost.

DE-RISK IT: Open the GROSHOP order export today and, in 10 minutes, count how many customers placed 2 or more orders in the last 12 months and how many orders contained 20 or more articles. If the 20+ article buyers number fewer than about 10, or repeat customers are a small share of orders, the reseller track and the loyalty base do not exist yet, and you should stop and drop GROFORCE.

IF FIX FIRST: Drop points, tiers and cards, and replace them with a WhatsApp price sheet for 20+ article buyers (written wholesale prices, in-country stock, certified after-sales). Test it on 10 bulk buyers and build nothing more unless 4 of 10 reply and 2 or more order within 7 days.
