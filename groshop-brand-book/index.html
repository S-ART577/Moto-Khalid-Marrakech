Brand foundation

GROSHOP is a Moroccan consumer-electronics retailer and wholesale platform, based in Marrakech, selling verified stock with its own after-sales service.

Vibe

Dark-first, cinematic, premium. Clean, modern, minimal, energetic, optimistic. Every design carries "Grow Beyond" energy: confidence, progress, opportunity.

Personality

Professional without coldness. Friendly without casualness. Ambitious, trustworthy, Moroccan.

Visual DNA, from the Facebook cover

  • Deep midnight navy with red light streaks bleeding across the frame
  • One huge, confident headline and nothing competing with it
  • Logo small and quiet, top-right
  • One supporting line anchored bottom-right
  • 70% or more of the canvas left to breathe

The rule behind every design

One hero message, cinematic darkness, disciplined restraint.

Rules

  • Headlines are huge and short: five words at most for a hero line. Negative space is part of the typography.
  • No serif fonts, no decorative fonts, no other families (no Sora, Urbanist or Space Grotesk).
  • All caps only for labels, badges and kickers.
  • Web body text never under 14px, line-height 1.5 or more.
  • Prices and stats use tabular figures so digits line up: font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums.

Backgrounds and campaign motifs

Every GROSHOP design starts dark. Daily work gets the quiet gradient; the four motifs are saved for moments that deserve them.

Daily · navy gradient 160°
Campaign · red light streaks
Campaign · ribbed glass
Campaign · orbs + grain

Decision rule

Daily post → gradient. Launch, campaign or cover → a motif. Unsure → gradient.

  • Daily: linear-gradient(160deg, #102533, #17344c). No texture. One soft radial glow behind the product is allowed.
  • Streaks: Midnight base, near-black corners. Red streaks heavily blurred, blend mode Screen, white-hot cores in the strongest ones. They enter from top-left and mid-right and leave the center clear for the headline.
  • Ribbed glass, orbs: always red on navy, never orange. Approved from the inspiration review.
  • Grain: overlay blend, 16% opacity at most.
  • Motifs are for launches, brand campaigns, covers, seasonal sales (Ramadan, Aïd, Black Friday) and milestones. Never behind dense text or product grids.
§8

Product treatment

Three treatments on dark backgrounds, in order of preference. Choose per product and context.

1 · Spotlight, the default

Product lit in the center, edges falling into navy darkness. Cinematic and premium.

2 · Cutout + soft shadow

Floating on the gradient with the product shadow. For packs, grids and catalog clarity.

3 · Red rim-light, hero only

Red edge light and glow. Launches with the streak motif. Use sparingly.

  • Premium photography only. No busy backgrounds inside the shot, no watermarks on product photos.
  • On single-product posts the product fills 45–65% of the canvas height.

Motion

Every GROSHOP button reads as one object being pushed: it lifts on approach, light sweeps across, it squashes on press, and its state resolves in place. Timings are tokens, never tuned by hand.

Magnet pulls up to 4px toward the pointer. The sheen crosses once per hover. The ripple starts where you click. "Ajouter au panier" keeps its width while the label turns into a spinner, then a drawn tick.

TokenValueUse
--dur-instant90msPress, release
--dur-fast160msColor swaps
--dur-base240msLift, slide, cross-fade
--dur-slow420msRipple, toggle pop
--dur-morph520msState morphs
--dur-sheen640msLight sweep
--ease-out.22,1,.36,1Arrivals
--ease-spring.34,1.56,.64,1Icons, toggles

  • Micro-interactions stay under 250ms so the UI never feels slow. Nothing loops on a resting UI.
  • Press scale: 0.97 for buttons, 0.92 for 44×44 icon buttons. Icon buttons are 44×44 minimum.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion: every animation drops to 1ms.
  • In video, something moves in the first half-second (a hand entering, a slide-in, a zoom), so a muted viewer sees that something is happening.

The two signature hooks

A hook is the first 1–2 seconds: the spoken line, the on-screen text and the first frame, together. Lead every batch with at least one of these.

Primary · Wait / Pause

"Wait… tqder tkhod ghi l-phone b [PRIX]…"
Pause 0.5s · silence · camera holds
"…wla takhod l-phone, l-écouteurs o l-chargeur, b nefs taman?"
نفس الثمن؟

The pause is the mechanism: the viewer prices item A, then the reveal breaks their own math. Never cut the silence.

Secondary · Fake checking

"Akha… hadchi ma ymkench ykoun s7i7. Khellini n3awed nchouf."
Visibly re-reads the price on the phone
"La… bqa s7i7. [OFFRE] b [PRIX]."
شفتها بعينيا

The check must be seen on camera. Use for humour, lifestyle and testimonial angles, and only on offers strong enough to justify the disbelief.

Other patterns: price reveal delay, warning or mistake, objection kill, direct callout, versus, curiosity gap, numbered list (three items), POV, proof or behind the scenes, speed challenge, reseller angle, testimonial, seasonal. In any set of eight hooks, use at least five patterns.

Sound off by default

  • On-screen text carries the hook in the first frame, not from 0:02.
  • The first frame is a thumbnail: design it like a poster.
  • Captions white or Cool Light Gray on navy, outside the top 220px and bottom 320px.

What kills a hook

  • Opening with a greeting or the brand name
  • Stating a feature instead of a stake
  • Fake urgency or invented scarcity
  • Tension that runs past six seconds

§15

Do and don't

The short version of every chapter.

Always

  • Start dark, on the gradient, unless there is a stated reason not to
  • One huge Degular headline, few words: Light for campaigns, Bold for promos
  • H-white-GS top-right on dark; the red arrow symbol on stories
  • Red for promo energy, white for premium calm, cyan as a whisper
  • 40% or more empty space, 8px grid, 72px margins, 9:16 safe zones
  • Canvas minimums: body 30, fine print 24, CTA 30
  • Darija-first copy; Arabic script on visuals, Arabizi in captions
  • Prices as 219,00 DH, true claims only

Never

  • Orange, gold, off-palette colors, rainbow or metallic gradients
  • Campaign motifs on daily posts or behind dense content
  • Two primary CTAs, cyan CTAs, red and cyan at equal weight
  • Stretched, recolored or effected logos; navy logos on dark
  • Navy-only symbol files on dark backgrounds
  • More than two weights, decorative or off-brand fonts
  • Thin or small white text on red; fine print under 65% opacity
  • Emoji in designed visuals; watermarks on product photos
§16

Workflow and QC

How Claude, Khalil and freelancers apply this book, in order, and the check every design passes before it ships.

Workflow

  1. Identify the asset type and pull its size and rules from §12.
  2. Pick the background: daily → gradient; campaign → confirm the motif if it is ambiguous (§7).
  3. Set the type: Degular headline, five words at most, weight by context; Urbane support; IBM Plex Sans Arabic for Darija; canvas sizes from §5.
  4. Place the logo: H-white-GS top-right, or the red arrow symbol on stories and tiny formats (§3).
  5. Treat the product: spotlight, then cutout, then rim-light (§8).
  6. CTA and badges: red or white logic, one primary, badges top-left, XXX,00 DH (§9, §13).
  7. Run the QC checklist.
  8. Scope discipline: deliver exactly what was asked; no unrequested redesign of shared elements.

QC checklist

  • Palette and derived tones only; no orange, no gold
  • Contrast pairs from the §4 table; red text only at large sizes
  • Text at or above the canvas minimums
  • 40% or more empty space, 8px grid, 72px margins
  • 9:16: nothing important in the top 220px or bottom 320px
  • Correct logo file for the background, top-right
  • Exactly one primary CTA
  • Prices formatted XXX,00 DH, claims true
  • Two font weights at most, hero weight by context
  • Darija checked by a native speaker when unsure
